How to fix a car scratch at home: a plain-English guide

Work out how deep the scratch is, pick the right product and avoid the mistakes that make it worse.

MW
Marcus Webb, Automotive Editor · Updated Oct 3, 2026 · 8 min read

THE SHORT ANSWER

Most light scratches only need a touch-up pen and a clear coat. If your fingernail catches in the scratch, it’s through the clear coat and needs more than polish.

Step 1: check how deep it is

Run a fingernail gently across the scratch. If it doesn’t catch, it’s in the clear coat and polish may remove it completely. If it catches, you’re into the color layer or primer, which requires a targeted color touch-up pen.

Step 2: clean and prep

Wash the damaged area thoroughly, dry it completely, and wipe with isopropyl alcohol or prep solvent. Fresh paint will not bond properly over road grime, carnauba wax, or silicone residue.

Step 3: pick the right product

For chips and fine scratches a touch-up pen is the quickest fix. See our roundup of the best touch-up paint pens for ranked options.

Step 4: apply in thin layers

Dab, don’t brush. Let each layer dry for 20 minutes before adding the next. Thin layers look far better than one thick blob that dries unevenly and creates a raised ridge.

Common mistakes

  • Applying in direct sunlight or on a hot vehicle panel.
  • Skipping the clear coat, which causes color pigment to wash away.
  • Ordering paint by general name rather than the exact factory OEM color code.
